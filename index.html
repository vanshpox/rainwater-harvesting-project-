Project Architecture & System Specifications

System design, hydraulic formulas, automated filtration mechanisms, and DROP optimization rules.

Problem Statement & Proposed System

Educational institutions in India encounter severe groundwater depletion, exorbitant reliance on private water tankers during summer months, and monsoon stormwater urban flooding. Traditional unmonitored systems suffer from clogged filters, overflowing storage sumps, and zero telemetry.

Key Architectural Innovations:
  • Automatic First-Flush Diverters: Mechanically isolates the first 1-2 mm of contaminated runoff carrying rooftop soot, leaves, and dust.
  • Dual-Chamber Filtration: Gravity-fed slow sand media + activated charcoal (GAC) removing suspended solids down to 5 microns.
  • Dynamic Spillway & Recharge Well: When RCC storage reservoir reaches 95% capacity, excess filtered runoff automatically diverts into an injection borewell to recharge deep aquifers.

Hydrological Governing Formulas

Derived from Central Ground Water Board (CGWB) & Bureau of Indian Standards IS: 15797:2008.

V = A × R × C × η

Where:
• V = Harvested Water Yield in Litres / year
• A = Catchment Area (m²) = Roof Area × Number of Blocks
• R = Mean Annual Precipitation in mm (1 mm over 1 m² = 1 Litre)
• C = Runoff Surface Coefficient (0.85 for concrete RCC)
• η = Filtration efficiency & first-flush factor (0.90 net yield)

Multi-Stage Filtration & First-Flush Diverter

Engineering schematic detailing coarse leaf deflection screen, buoyant floating ball valve chamber for dirty initial wash, graded silica sand & activated charcoal filter bed, and dual-outlet diversion to underground RCC sump and groundwater injection well.

Campus Retention Reservoir & IoT Sensor Array

College Viva & Examination Prep Center

Frequently asked viva voce questions with comprehensive data-driven and hydrological engineering explanations.

The governing equation is V = A × R × C × η. Mathematically, 1 mm = 0.001 m depth of precipitation. Over an area of 1 m², the volume of water equals 1 m² × 0.001 m = 0.001 m³. Since 1 m³ = 1,000 Litres, 0.001 m³ × 1,000 = exactly 1 Litre! Hence, depth in millimeters directly translates to Litres per square meter.
During dry spells, rooftops accumulate soot, industrial particulate matter, bird droppings, and organic matter. The first-flush diverter automatically traps and discards the initial 1 to 2 mm of precipitation (approximately 20 to 25 Litres per 100 m² of roof area). Once the vertical diversion pipe fills, an internal floating rubber ball seals the chamber inlet, allowing subsequent clean rainwater to flow into the primary storage reservoir.
Even impermeable concrete roofs experience hydrological losses: depression storage (small puddles on flat roofs), initial absorption into weathered concrete pores, wind drift, and evaporation before water reaches roof drains. According to Bureau of Indian Standards IS 15797:2008, Reinforced Cement Concrete (RCC) retains a runoff coefficient of 0.80 to 0.85. Smooth corrugated metal roofs have higher coefficients (0.90), while clay tiles average 0.75 to 0.80.
The score evaluates four 25-point weighted engineering pillars: (1) Catchment Planning (total roof coverage across academic and hostel blocks), (2) Hydrological Efficiency (surface quality and regional precipitation abundance), (3) Storage Tank Adequacy (evaluating whether storage capacity matches 12% to 35% of annual harvest to avoid overflow wastage while maintaining economic tank construction cost), and (4) Water Offset & Self-Sufficiency (ratio of harvested yield against campus non-potable demand). Total scores ≥ 90 earn 'Platinum Green Campus' rating.
Under Central Ground Water Authority (CGWA) and State Municipal By-Laws (such as Bruhat Bengaluru Mahanagara Palike BBMP and Chennai Metro Water), all educational institutions having roof area > 1,000 m² or plot area > 5,000 m² are legally mandated to install rainwater harvesting and recharge systems. The project adheres to IS 15797:2008 ('Roof Top Rainwater Harvesting — Guidelines') and Central Public Health and Environmental Engineering Organisation (CPHEEO) manuals.
Recharging must never directly pump untreated surface water into an aquifer. Before reaching the recharge well, water passes through: (a) Leaf filter screen, (b) First-flush separation chamber, (c) A slow gravity filtration pit containing graded coarse sand (0.5 - 1.0 mm), coarse gravel (2 - 5 mm), and activated charcoal to adsorb dissolved hydrocarbons and heavy metals. Recharging is only conducted through slotted PVC casing pipes anchored into permeable geological strata.
